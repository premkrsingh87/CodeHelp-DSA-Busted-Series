How it works

Three moves. No edit bay.

The script is the source of truth from the first word to the final render. Nothing gets generated that a line didn't ask for.

STEP 01

Write it, or paste it.

A finished screenplay, a VO transcript, a rough outline, or three bullets. Plain English is a valid script. Drop in your own narration and ScriptCut cuts to your voice instead of generating one.

STEP 02

Five passes, in parallel.

Research verifies the claims. Casting sources footage per line. Edit times the cuts to the read. Sound design scores and mixes. Grade unifies the look. Roughly 38 minutes for a 20-minute master.

STEP 03

Direct the revision.

"Tighten the cold open." "Swap the B-roll on line 14." Say it in the chat and only that stretch re-renders — or open the timeline and move the clips yourself. Nothing else changes.

The editor

Every clip knows which line it came from.

Click a line of script and the timeline lights up the frames it produced. Rewrite the line and only those frames re-cut. That binding is the whole product.

Questions

The ones people actually ask.

That fear is what the product was built against. Nothing comes from a template: every cut is timed to your read, every clip is real footage sourced for that specific line, and the score is written for your script. It gets you about 80% of the way there and the editor covers the rest. Watch a full render before you decide.

That's the intended path. Paste a finished script up to 40 minutes, upload narration as MP3 or WAV, and drop in your own footage — ScriptCut cuts to your voice instead of generating one. Bringing your own VO doesn't lower the per-minute cost, since that covers the whole pipeline rather than just the narration.

Tell the agent in plain English, or open the timeline yourself. Because every clip is bound to the line that produced it, re-cutting a stretch of script only touches those frames — the parts you already approved stay exactly as they are. You never start over from a prompt.

Long-form documentary, explainer, breakdown and ranked-list formats, six to forty minutes, in English. It works by sourcing real footage, so it is strongest where that footage exists: history, finance, true crime, science, geopolitics, machines and transport. It is not built for vlogs, gaming, reactions, software tutorials, or vertical formats. The test: if there are twenty-plus minutes of real footage findable online for your subject, it will cut it well.

You own the master, the project file, and the score stems. Every clip carries its licence and origin in an exportable manifest, and you set the sourcing policy per channel — lock it to public domain and Creative Commons if that's your risk tolerance.
